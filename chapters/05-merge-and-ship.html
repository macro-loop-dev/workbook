Merge and ship

main is the live site. Merging is deploying. There is no separate "publish" step, and there never will be.

Why this chapter exists

Until now your site has lived in two places: the folder on your laptop and the copy on GitHub. Nobody could visit either. At the last checkpoint William connected a third place, Cloudflare Pages, and told it one thing: whenever the main branch changes, copy it to the internet. That sentence is the whole of "deploying". Deploy means putting a version of the site where other people's browsers can reach it.

Your chapter-4 pull request is still open, approved but not merged. When you press Merge, main changes, Cloudflare notices, and a minute later your phone can see it. Every chapter from here on ends with that minute.

A story

FloatNote's DEPLOYMENT.md, 2026-08-23

FloatNote went live on the web at floatnote.dev. The next day William moved it to app.floatnote.dev. Not because anything was broken: because a browser ties everything it remembers for a site (saved notes, an installed app icon, offline files) to the exact address, which is called the origin. Move the address and all of that is left behind. With zero users the move cost one afternoon. With a thousand users it would have cost each of them their notes. Decisions about addresses get expensive fast, which is why the next chapter, the one where you pick your address, comes right after this one.

What you will do

  • Merge your chapter-4 pull request on GitHub and read what the button says.
  • Watch Cloudflare notice, copy, and finish, in its dashboard.
  • Open the live site on your phone.
  • Bring your laptop's main up to date and delete the finished branch.
  • Do the whole loop again for chapter 5, this time also looking at the preview address.

Do it

  1. Open your pull request. In PowerShell 7, inside the repo: 
    cd ~\dev\workbook; gh pr view chapter-4 --web
    Your browser opens the PR. At the bottom: a green tick from William's review, a green tick from the gitleaks check, and a green Merge pull request button.
  2. Look at the live site first. Open the address that ends in .pages.dev. It is printed at the top of the project's page in the Cloudflare dashboard, and William has it written down.

    A lower number than your laptop shows, probably 0 of 12 done. The live site is built from main, and none of your commits are on main yet. They are all on the branch, waiting.

  3. Merge. Click Merge pull request. The button offers only one kind, Create a merge commit: William turned the others off. A merge commit is an ordinary commit with two parents, "main as it was" and "your branch as it ended". Click Confirm merge.

    Purple "Merged" badge. Click Delete branch: that is GitHub's copy of chapter-4 only. Your laptop's copy is step 8.

  4. Watch it deploy. In the Cloudflare dashboard (menus as of 2026-09-02; William has the click paths in his for-william/dashboard-clicks.md): Workers & Pages → workbook → Deployments. A new row appears at the top, labelled Production, with your merge commit's message next to it. Its status goes Queued → Building → Success. Click the row to read the log.

    About a minute. The log says there was no build command and it uploaded your files as they are. That is correct until chapter 8.

  5. Refresh the live site. Same .pages.dev address.

    5 of 12 done. Chapters 0 to 4, exactly what your laptop says. What happened in between: GitHub told Cloudflare that main moved; Cloudflare fetched the repo, took the files, and copied them to its servers in many countries. That network of copies is a CDN, and it is why the site is fast from anywhere.

  6. Open it on your phone. Type the same address. Any network, not just home Wi-Fi.

    5 of 12. Your phone has never seen your laptop's folder. This is the first time the site exists somewhere you did not put it.

  7. Bring your laptop up to date. Your laptop's main does not know about the merge yet. 
    git switch main; git pull

    A few lines ending in Fast-forward, then a list of changed files. Fast-forward means your main simply moved ahead to where GitHub's is, because it was a straight line: nothing on your side that GitHub did not already have.

  8. Look at the shape of it. 
    git log --oneline --graph -6

    The top line is Merge pull request #N from ..., with a fork in the drawing under it where your branch joins. Then:

    git branch -d chapter-4

    Deleted branch chapter-4 (was abc1234). The commits are still there, inside main. A branch is only a name pointing at a commit; you deleted the name.

Check

Three things, in this order:

git status
git branch
git log --oneline -3

git status says On branch main and Your branch is up to date with 'origin/main'. git branch lists only main. The top of the log is the merge commit. And the .pages.dev address, on the laptop and on the phone, says 5 of 12 done.

What can go wrong

  • The Merge button is grey. Read the text next to it. Either the review is missing (William has not approved this version) or a check is still running (yellow dot). Both are the protection William set up in chapter 4 doing its job. Wait, or ask.
  • git pull says "Not possible to fast-forward" or "You have divergent branches". GitHub merged in a way that rewrote your commits (a setting on the repo, not something you did). Stop. Do not type git pull --rebase or anything with --force because a web page suggested it. Ask William; the fix is his, and it is two commands.
  • Deployment says Success but the site is old. Press Ctrl+F5 for a hard refresh, which ignores the browser's saved copy. Still old? Wait one minute. The CDN's copies update one by one and the one near you can be last.
  • Deployment says Failed. Open the log. If it says the output directory dist was not found, the build settings were changed early (that directory only exists from chapter 8). William fixes it in the dashboard; nothing in your files is wrong.
  • You merged but the counter did not move. Look at the address bar. If there is a branch name at the front, like chapter-5.something.pages.dev, you are on a preview, not production. Previews are explained in Mark it done, step 4.
  • Your phone shows an older version than your laptop. It is on a different network and talks to a different copy. Wait a minute and pull down to refresh.
  • git branch -d refuses: "not fully merged". You skipped git pull. Pull, then delete. Do not use capital -D to make the message go away; it deletes without checking.

Mark it done

The loop from chapter 4, plus the deploy at the end. This is the loop for the rest of the course.

  1. A branch for this chapter: 
    git switch -c chapter-5
  2. In index.html, on the data-chapter="05" line, change data-status="todo" to "done". By hand or by asking the AI; check the diff either way.
  3. Commit and push: 
    git add index.html; git commit -m "docs: mark chapter 5 done"; git push -u origin chapter-5
gh pr create --fill
  4. Look at the preview. Within a minute a Cloudflare comment appears on the PR with a link like https://chapter-5.<project>.pages.dev. Open it.

    6 of 12. This is a preview: Cloudflare deployed your branch to its own throwaway address so you can see what main would look like. Nobody's bookmark points at it. The real address, with no branch name in front, still says 5.

  5. Wait for William's approval. Merge, delete the branch on GitHub, watch the Deployments list, refresh the real address.

    6 of 12, on the laptop and on the phone.

  6. Tidy up: 
    git switch main; git pull; git branch -d chapter-5
  7. One line in Today I learned, at the top. Commit it on your next branch; it does not need its own PR.

Pair with William

You will: say, in your own words, what happens between clicking Merge and your phone showing the new number. Then: what is the difference between the production address and a preview address, and which one would you give a friend?

William will: open the Deployments list, pick the previous deployment, and click Rollback. Refresh the live site: 5 of 12 again, and nothing in git changed. Then he re-deploys the latest. Every deployment is kept; going back is a click, which is why shipping is not scary.

Deliberately not in this chapter

No custom address: that is chapter 6, and the story above is why it gets its own chapter. No build step: Cloudflare copies your files as they are until chapter 8. No gh pr merge from the terminal, although it exists: the button shows the choices and the command hides them.
